Common Website Problems That Cost Roofing Contractors Money:

Most roofing websites are leaking leads regularly because of the same fixable mistakes. Here are the five we see most often, and how to address each one.

A. Confusing Navigation

If a homeowner can’t find your services, service area, or phone number within a few seconds, they leave. Roofing websites with messy menus and buried contact info lose clients on a daily basis, including the high-value storm damage calls that come in fast and go to whoever answers first.

Simplify Your Menu: Use clear labels: Home, Services, Service Areas, Gallery, About, Reviews, Contact. Add a sticky phone number and ‘Free Estimate’ button at the top of every page so homeowners can reach you instantly.

B. Not Mobile-Friendly

Most roofing searches happen on phones, especially after storms. If your site doesn’t adjust to a phone screen, buttons are too small, or text is hard to read, you’re pushing away a majority of your potential customers at an inopportune time.

Go Mobile-First: Build your site for phones first, then scale up for desktops. Test every page on multiple devices. Make sure your phone number and estimate button are easy to tap on any screen, even in the rain.

C. Outdated Design

An outdated website makes homeowners question the quality of your work. 70% of roofing companies are unhappy with their current web presence. If your site looks like it was built years ago, visitors click the back button and call someone whose site looks current and professional.

Refresh Your Look: Update with a modern layout, professional project photos, drone shots of completed roofs, and clean branding. A fresh site tells homeowners your roofing business is active, capable, and worth calling.

D. No Google Rankings (Local SEO Missing)

Without local SEO, your site fails to appear when someone in your city looks up a roofer. You could be the best contractor in town, but if Google doesn’t know that, your phone stays quiet while your competitors’ phones ring.

Set Up Local SEO: Add your city and service area to page titles, headings, and content. Claim your Google Business Profile and keep it updated with photos, completed projects, and customer reviews after every job.

E. Weak Calls to Action

Some roofing websites have great photos but never ask the visitor to do anything. No ‘Get a Free Estimate‘ button. No ‘Schedule Your Inspection‘ form. Without straightforward calls to action, homeowners browse your site and leave without ever reaching out.

Add CTAs to Every Page: Place a ‘Get a Free Estimate‘ or ‘Schedule Your Roof Inspection‘ button on every page. Use urgent, action-driven language that matches what homeowners need: ‘Get Your Free Estimate Today’ or ‘Book Your Storm Damage Inspection.’

Must-Have Pages for Every Roofing Contractor Website:

A roofing website needs specific pages to serve homeowners and show up on Google. Ones with only a home page and a contact form won’t compete in a local market where homeowners have dozens of options a click away.

Here are the pages every roofing contractor website should have before going live.

Home Page

Your digital front door. Feature your best roofing photos, top services, star rating, and a clear ‘Get a Free Estimate’ button. Make the first impression count every time a homeowner lands on your site.

Services Page

Create separate pages for each major service: roof replacement, roof repair, storm damage repair, new construction roofing, commercial roofing, and gutter installation. Individual pages help improve rank for more specific searches and capture additional types of leads.

Service Area Page

Tell Google and your customers exactly where you work. Label every city, county, and zip code in your coverage area so homeowners know you serve their neighborhood.

Before and After Gallery

Demonstrate real project photos. Include different roof types, materials, and project sizes. Drone photography makes a stunning visual impact and builds immediate credibility.

About Page

Share your story, years of experience, licensing, insurance, manufacturer certifications, and team bios. Homeowners want to know who they’re trusting with their significant investment.

Reviews and Testimonials

Display your best Google reviews. 35% of homeowners say online reviews are their top factor in selecting a roofer, and that number keeps growing. Make your reviews easy to find and prominent on every key page.

Contact Page

Include a form, phone number, email, and a map. Make it impossible for someone to miss how to reach you. Every extra click between interest and contact costs you estimate requests.

Blog

Publish helpful articles on topics like ‘How to Spot Storm Damage on Your Roof’ or ‘How Long Does a Roof Replacement Take?’ This helps your SEO and builds authority with homeowners doing research before calling.

FAQ Page

Answer common questions homeowners ask before hiring a roofing contractor. This also helps your site resonate in Google’s featured snippets for question-based searches.

Storm Damage and Insurance Claims Page

A crucial component for roofing contractors. Storm damage repairs make up a huge portion of roofing work. A dedicated page explaining your storm damage inspection process, insurance claim assistance, and emergency response helps you capture high-value leads every time severe weather hits your area.

How to Pick the Right Web Designer for Your Roofing Business:

Choosing the wrong web designer wastes your time and money. Not every designer understands the roofing industry, the urgency of storm-season leads, or what it takes to rank in competitive local markets.

Here are six things to look for before you hire anyone.

A. They Have Built Roofing Contractor Websites Before

A designer who has worked with roofers understands your seasonal demand, storm-driven lead surges, and the types of content that convert visitors into estimate requests. Inquire about professional samples of roofing websites they’ve built.

B. They Know Your Local Market

Your designer should understand how homeowners in your area find roofers. They need to set up service area pages for every city you cover and optimize for ‘near me‘ searches and storm-season spikes in your specific market.

C. They Have Strong Reviews from Other Roofing Contractors

Check their Google reviews and testimonials. Look for feedback from roofers or home service contractors mentioning specific outcomes: greater estimate requests, better rankings, or more signed contracts after launch.

D. They Start by Learning About Your Business

A remarkable designer asks about your most profitable services, your storm season, your insurance work, and your goals before they design anything. If they skip discovery and go straight to templates, that’s a warning sign.

E. They Give You a Clear, Itemized Contract

You should know exactly what you’re paying for, whether it’s design, development, SEO, hosting, timeline, and site ownership. Avoid anyone vague about deliverables. You should own your domain outright immediately.

F. They Follow a Proven Process

Learn their workflow from discovery to launch. A reliable designer has a defined step-by-step process. Anyone who says they’ll figure it out as they go is a risk to your timeline and your budget.

FAQs About Roofing Contractor Website Marketing

A. Should I use Google Ads alongside my website, or is SEO enough?

Both work best together, but they serve different purposes. SEO cements your long-term visibility alongside delivering leads even when you’re not spending. Google Ads provides an immediate boost for competitive searches or storm-season surges when you need leads fast. Most roofing contractors start with a pristine site and SEO foundation, then layer in paid ads as their budget grows.

B. Do I need a separate page for every city I serve, or will one service area page cover it?

You need a separate page for each major city or county you target. Google ranks individual pages, not your site as a whole. A single ‘We serve the greater metro area‘ page won’t rank for ‘roofer in Naperville‘ or ‘roof repair in Schaumburg.’ Dedicated service area pages, each with local content and keywords, are  quick ways to expand your Google footprint across your whole coverage area.

C. How long does it take before a new roofing website starts bringing in leads?

You can start gaining leads from day one if you combine your launch with Google Ads or a well-optimized Google Business Profile. Organic SEO typically takes 3 to 6 months to build momentum. Most roofing contractors see a meaningful increase in organic leads between months four and six, with results compounding from there as you add content and reviews.

D. Can't I just use a website builder like Wix or Squarespace instead?

You can, but it comes with real trade-offs. Website builders are easy to set up but difficult to optimize for local SEO. They limit your ability to build custom service area pages, add structured data for FAQ schema, and integrate roofing-specific tools like estimate calculators. Contractors who switch from a DIY builder to a purpose-built WordPress site consistently report more organic traffic and more estimate requests within the first year.

E. Should my roofing website show prices, or is it better to keep them off?
